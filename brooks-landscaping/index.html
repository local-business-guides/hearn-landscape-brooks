Begin your Brooks landscaping search with questions

When you contact a landscaper, explain what’s prompting the project. You might be preparing a yard for new sod, dealing with uneven ground, or thinking about an outdoor area that needs a clearer layout. A few photos and notes can help you describe the issue, but an on-site look is useful for understanding the property itself.

Ask the contractor to explain the proposed work in everyday terms. Which parts of the yard are included? Is preparation needed before installation? Are there choices you’ll need to make about materials or layout? For Brooks landscaping, those details help you understand what the estimate covers and what you should expect during the project.

Prepare a useful estimate checklist

A little preparation can keep the first conversation focused. Before the visit, consider:

  • What you want changed, and which goal comes first.
  • Where you notice water collecting, soil shifting or grass failing to grow well.
  • How people currently use the yard and where access is needed.
  • Whether you’re considering sod, a retaining wall, grading, pavers, a patio or another service.
  • Any preferences you already have for the look or function of the space.
  • What questions you have about communication from estimate to completion.

You don’t need to choose every detail on your own. It’s more helpful to be clear about the problem you want solved and the way you hope to use the finished area. Watch for gaps before work starts

A common mistake is assuming that a feature name tells you everything included in a proposal. Ask whether the ground needs to be graded, how drainage concerns will be handled, and what preparation is part of the work. For sod, Hearn Landscape prepares the yard before installation and provides care tips for the months after the job.

It’s also easy to focus on the visible finish and overlook how the work will connect to the rest of the property. If a project includes more than one improvement, ask how the elements are planned together and which decisions need to be settled first. Make sure you know who to contact if a question comes up while the work is underway.

Know when professional help makes sense

Call a landscaper when the project involves changing the grade, managing drainage, building a retaining wall, or coordinating several types of work. Professional input can also help if you’re not sure why a part of the yard stays wet or whether the ground is ready for sod. Early advice may help you avoid beginning with a surface fix when the underlying condition needs attention.
