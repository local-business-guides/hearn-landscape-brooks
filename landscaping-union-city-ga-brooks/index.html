What should landscaping include?

Landscaping can be a targeted project, such as installing sod or refreshing a planting bed, or a coordinated update to several parts of the yard. Before work starts, agree on the areas included, the materials and plants planned, and what the finished result should look like.

A written scope may cover site preparation, planting or turf installation, bed shaping, and cleanup, depending on the job. Ask whether hauling away old materials, irrigation changes, drainage work, edging, and follow-up care are included or would need a separate agreement.

What should a local professional check at the property?

A useful assessment starts with the parts of the yard you want to change and how you use them. The professional should look at existing plants and turf, sun and shade, slope, water movement, access for materials, and any features that need to stay in place.

For a sod project, ask how the existing surface will be prepared and how the selected turf fits the site conditions. For planting beds, discuss mature plant size, spacing, and maintenance needs so the design makes sense beyond the day it is installed.

  • Point out areas where water collects or runs toward a building.
  • Show the professional utility equipment, walkways, trees, or other features that should be protected.
  • Explain how much time you want to spend on upkeep after installation.

What changes the cost of landscaping?

Price depends on the work itself, the area involved, and what the property needs before installation. Removing existing turf or plants, preparing uneven ground, moving materials through a tight access point, or addressing water movement can add labor or materials to the scope.

Plant and turf selections, bed size, edging, disposal, and any irrigation or drainage work can also change the estimate. Ask for a written breakdown that names the work and materials, notes exclusions, and explains how a change to the plan would be approved. No estimate should be compared fairly with another until you know what each one includes.

What can affect the start date and project timing?

Timing depends on the project’s scope, material availability, crew scheduling, site access, and conditions on the property. Rain or saturated ground can affect when some preparation and installation work can be done, while a project with several phases may take longer than a single focused update.

Ask for an expected start window and a realistic description of the work sequence rather than relying on a verbal promise of an exact finish date. If the plan depends on a particular plant or turf selection, ask what happens if that material is unavailable.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

The most common trouble starts with mismatched expectations. A general description like “fix the yard” doesn’t tell you which beds, surfaces, or cleanup tasks are included, and a low estimate may leave out preparation or disposal that another provider has included.

  • Don’t assume lawn mowing and landscape installation are the same service.
  • Don’t approve plant or turf selections without discussing site conditions and future care.
  • Don’t overlook water flow, access, or items that need to be protected during work.
  • Don’t rely on a review score alone; check the scope and ask how questions or changes will be handled.
