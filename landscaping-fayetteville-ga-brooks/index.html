What should landscaping include?

Landscaping is work that changes or maintains the planted and usable parts of a property. A project might involve a new planting plan, refreshing existing beds, adding sod, or bringing several areas together so the yard feels more consistent. The right scope depends on what you want to change, the condition of the site, and how much upkeep you want afterward.

Before work begins, the proposal should describe the areas included and the tasks planned for each one. Ask it to identify materials, labor, cleanup, and any work that is not included. If design is part of the job, confirm how the plan and any revisions will be reviewed before materials are ordered.

What should a local professional check at the property?

A useful assessment starts with your goals, then looks at the conditions that could affect the plan. The professional should walk the areas under consideration and note slopes, existing plants, access for equipment, and places where water collects or runs across the yard. Those observations help shape a scope that fits the property instead of relying on a generic design.

Discuss how you use the yard and what you want to keep. A play area, outdoor seating, shade, privacy, and low-maintenance planting can call for different choices. If the project includes sod or new beds, ask how existing conditions will be prepared and what care the new work will need after installation.
