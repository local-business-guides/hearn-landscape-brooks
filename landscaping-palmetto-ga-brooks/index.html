What should landscaping in Brooks include?

Landscaping is broader than routine mowing. Depending on the property and your goals, it can include planning planting areas, selecting plants, installing sod, shaping beds, or improving how people move through an outdoor space.

A project might address one bare or awkward section, or cover several parts of the yard. Before comparing proposals, ask each provider to describe the specific areas and tasks included, along with the plants or materials they expect to use.

What should a local professional check at the property?

A useful first visit starts with the site, not a one-size-fits-all package. The professional should look at the existing lawn and planting areas, slopes, paths, access for equipment, and any spots where water collects or runs across the property.

They should also ask what you want the space to do, what needs to stay, and how much upkeep you prefer. If the work could affect drainage, boundaries, underground utilities, or shared areas, raise those concerns early so the scope can account for them.

  • Point out the exact areas to change and anything that should remain untouched.
  • Share any known drainage trouble, past planting issues, or access limits.
  • Ask how the proposed materials and layout fit the conditions they observed.

What changes the cost of landscaping?

There isn't enough information to give a reliable price without seeing the job and defining the work. Cost can change with the size and condition of the area, the amount of preparation needed, the materials and plants selected, and whether the project includes design, sod, or several kinds of work.

Access and removal can matter too. A hard-to-reach section, existing material that needs to be cleared, or site conditions that call for extra preparation may change the amount of labor involved. Ask for a written scope that separates included work from optional items, so proposals are easier to compare.

  • How much ground needs preparation or removal?
  • Which plants, sod, or other materials are included?
  • Does the proposal include design or only installation?
  • Are cleanup, delivery, or work outside the named areas included?

What can affect the project schedule?

Timing depends on the size of the job, the condition of the site, material availability, and the work sequence. Weather can also delay outdoor work, especially when the ground is too wet for equipment or installation.

Ask for an estimated start window and the expected order of work rather than relying on a single finish date. If the plan depends on selected plants or sod, ask when those materials will be confirmed and what happens if availability changes.

What mistakes should you avoid before work begins?

A common source of frustration is agreeing on a general idea without agreeing on the details. If one person expects a planting bed and another expects sod, the difference should be settled before work starts.

Don't assume that cleanup, disposal, or work beyond the main project area is automatically included. Have the provider identify the work area, materials, exclusions, and process for approving changes in writing.

Also avoid overlooking local requirements. Rules can depend on the exact parcel, the type of work, and any applicable association requirements. Check with the relevant local office or association when a project may involve drainage, grading, boundaries, or shared property.
