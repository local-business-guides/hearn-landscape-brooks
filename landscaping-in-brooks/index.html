Set priorities for landscaping in brooks

Before picking plants or settling on a new lawn, think about the routines your yard should support. Do you want more room for outdoor meals, a clearer path across the property, or a larger stretch of grass? Choose the changes that matter most now, and note any ideas you may want to save for a later phase.

Walk the property at different times of day if you can. Notice which areas you use, where you’d like a sitting spot, and whether a planned feature might block a route you rely on. When you discuss landscaping in brooks with a professional, these everyday details help shape a plan that suits your home rather than just one part of the yard.

Prepare for an estimate

You don't need a finished design before asking for help. A few notes and a walk around the property are enough to begin a useful conversation. Point out areas where the ground seems uneven, places where water causes concern, and any features that should remain untouched.

Bring up the order you have in mind, too. Sod installation, grading, drainage changes, irrigation, pavers and patios may need to be considered as parts of one overall project. Hearn Landscape can discuss landscape design and the services that fit your plans. The team prepares a yard before laying sod and shares care tips for the months ahead.

Use this checklist before the visit:

  • Mark the parts of the yard you hope to change.
  • List the features you want to keep or work around.
  • Think about how you use the space and what you'd like to do there.
  • Write down questions about site preparation, timing and care after the work.

Plan the work in a sensible order

A common planning mistake is to focus only on the final surface. A smooth lawn, for example, depends on the condition and preparation of the ground underneath. If the project includes a patio, retaining wall or drainage work, talk about how those pieces relate before settling on where the sod will go.

It's also easy to add features without checking how they'll work together. A walkway should connect the places you actually go, and a gathering area should fit the way you expect to use it. Share your priorities early so the design conversation can account for the whole property, not just a single feature.
