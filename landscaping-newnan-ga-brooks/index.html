What should landscaping in Brooks include?

Landscaping can mean a focused improvement, such as refreshing planting beds, or several connected changes across the yard. Depending on the property and your goals, the work may cover design, plant selection, bed preparation, sod, grading, drainage, edging, mulch, or cleanup.

A clear proposal should identify the areas being worked on, the materials and plants included, and any preparation needed before installation. It should also say what is excluded, how leftover material and debris will be handled, and whether you need to take care of anything after the crew leaves.

What should a local pro check at the property?

A site visit should look at more than the appearance of the lawn. The professional should note slopes, low spots, drainage patterns, sun and shade, existing trees and plants, soil condition, and how people use the yard. Those details can change which plants or materials make sense.

They should also ask what you want to solve first. A bare patch, water collecting near the house, a hard-to-maintain bed, and a desire for new planting are different problems, and they may call for different scopes of work. Point out any areas that stay wet or dry out quickly, and mention where you want to preserve existing plants.

Before digging or changing grades, confirm how underground utilities and any property or neighborhood requirements will be checked. Depending on the work, permits or homeowners association approval may also be relevant, so ask who is responsible for confirming those requirements.

What affects landscaping cost and timing?

There isn't one standard price for landscaping because the work depends on the property and the decisions made for it. The size of the work area, site access, slope, soil preparation, drainage needs, removal of existing material, plant or sod selection, and the amount of labor can all affect a quote. A design, installation, and material supply may also be priced or described as separate parts of the project.

Timing depends on the scope, the condition of the site, material availability, weather, and the contractor's schedule. Work that requires grading or drainage changes can take a different amount of preparation than a straightforward bed refresh. Ask for an expected start window, an estimate of the work duration, and an explanation of what could delay either one.

For a useful comparison, ask each provider to quote the same work area and list the same materials or tasks. A lower total may reflect a narrower scope, less site preparation, or different materials, so compare the written details rather than the number alone.

What mistakes should you avoid?

One common mistake is choosing plants or sod before checking the conditions where they will go. Sun exposure, drainage, and the existing grade affect how well a plan fits the yard. Another is treating a symptom, such as a soggy area, without asking what is causing it.

Avoid relying on a verbal description of the work. Make sure the written scope names the areas, preparation, materials, cleanup, and any work that is not included. If the proposal changes after the site visit, ask for the revised details in writing before approving the change.

Don't assume routine lawn service covers landscape installation, or that a design plan includes the labor and materials to build it. Ask directly which tasks the provider will perform and which, if any, you'll need to arrange separately.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which parts of my yard and which tasks are included in this proposal?
  • What site preparation, removal, grading, or drainage work is included?
  • Which plants, sod, and other materials are specified, and can you show me those choices?
  • Who will handle utility checks and confirm whether local or neighborhood approvals apply?
  • What is the expected schedule, and what conditions could change it?
  • What cleanup is included at the end of the work?
  • What care will the new plants or sod need after installation?
  • How are changes to the scope or materials documented and approved?

When should you call a landscaping pro?

Call a professional when you want several parts of the yard to work together, when a slope or drainage issue is involved, or when you are planning sod, planting, or substantial bed changes. A site review can help distinguish a design preference from a condition that needs to be addressed before installation.

If you are mainly looking for recurring mowing or simple upkeep, ask a lawn service what it covers before requesting a landscape installation quote. If you are unsure which type of provider fits, describe the problem and the outcome you want, then ask what work they recommend and why.
