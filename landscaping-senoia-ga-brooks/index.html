What should landscaping in Senoia and Brooks include?

Landscaping is the planning, installation, or improvement of outdoor areas. Depending on your goals, a project may include a landscape design, planting beds, shrubs or trees, sod, grading, or drainage work. It can also mean updating parts of an existing yard rather than starting from scratch.

Start with the problem you want to solve. A front yard might need a more welcoming entry and planting layout, while a backyard may need a more usable lawn area or better handling of runoff. Ask for a written scope that identifies what will be installed, what is not included, and how the crew will handle cleanup.

What should a local professional check at the property?

A useful assessment starts with the ground and how water moves across it. The professional should look for low spots, slopes, places where runoff collects, and areas where water may flow toward the house. Existing trees, roots, shade, and the condition of the lawn can also affect which plants or materials make sense.

The visit should cover how you use the space and what you want to change. Point out gates, paths, outdoor living areas, and places that need to stay accessible. Before digging or changing grades, clarify property lines and ask who will check for underground utilities or determine whether any approvals are needed.

A clear proposal should reflect what the provider saw, rather than simply list standard services. If the recommendation includes new sod or planting, ask how the site will be prepared and how the plan accounts for drainage, shade, and existing features.

What changes the cost of a landscaping project?

There isn't one reliable price for landscaping without knowing the property and the requested work. A project that involves design, removing existing materials, preparing the ground, or changing drainage usually has a different scope from planting a few new shrubs or refreshing a small bed.

  • The size and shape of the area, along with access for people and equipment.
  • The amount of clearing, removal, grading, or soil preparation needed.
  • The kinds and quantities of plants, sod, and other materials selected.
  • Whether the plan includes drainage changes, new beds, paths, or several connected work areas.
  • How much of the existing landscape can stay and how much must be removed or protected.

Ask each provider to describe the same scope when you compare proposals. Check whether disposal, site preparation, materials, installation, and cleanup are included, and ask how a change in conditions would be handled. That makes it easier to compare what you're actually paying for without relying on a bare total.

How long can landscaping in Senoia take?

Timing depends on the size of the job, the work involved, the provider's schedule, and the availability of materials. A straightforward installation is different from a project that needs a design, several work stages, or adjustments to the site before planting or sod can go in.

Weather and ground conditions can also affect outdoor work. Ask when the provider expects to begin, what milestones are part of the plan, and what might cause a delay. If timing matters for a move, event, or other project, mention that early and get realistic expectations rather than assuming a start date is a completion date.

Which landscape choices matter most for a Brooks yard?

A good design should fit the way you use the yard and the conditions at your home. It should account for sunny and shaded areas, existing trees, drainage patterns, and how much upkeep you want to take on. A planting plan that looks appealing on paper may not be the right fit if the site conditions or maintenance needs don't match.

  • For a more usable lawn, discuss sod, site preparation, and water movement before choosing the work.
  • For a refreshed entry, consider how planting beds, shrubs, and paths work together.
  • For a yard with recurring wet areas, ask for an assessment of the water issue before adding new plants or lawn.
  • For a lower-maintenance plan, explain how much regular care you want to do and ask how that affects the design.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

The most common problem is agreeing to a vague scope. If one proposal includes soil preparation and removal while another only covers installation, the totals won't be a fair comparison. Get the work and exclusions in writing, including who supplies materials and what happens to debris.

Don't choose plants, sod, or layout without discussing site conditions and the care they require. Also avoid treating a drainage symptom as a cosmetic issue: adding soil or planting over a wet area may not address the underlying flow of water. Ask the provider to explain the reasoning behind the recommended work.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What work is included in the proposal, and what is excluded?
  • Who will handle design, installation, and any work outside your team's scope?
  • How will you assess drainage, shade, and soil or lawn conditions?
  • What preparation is needed before planting or installing sod?
  • Are materials, removal, and cleanup included in the written scope?
  • What could change the cost or schedule after work begins?
  • Do I need to check local rules, property lines, utility locations, or HOA requirements before the work?
  • What care will the new plants or sod need after installation?
