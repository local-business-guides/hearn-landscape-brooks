What to sort out before you call

Start with the changes you want to see, not a list of materials. Maybe water collects near the house, a slope is hard to use, or the lawn needs new sod. Write down what is bothering you and what you hope will be different when the work is done. Photos of the area can help explain the situation, especially if the issue changes with rain or seasons.

A local landscaper Brooks homeowners hire should be able to discuss the work in plain terms. Ask what the project includes, what needs to happen before installation, and which decisions you’ll need to make. For a larger plan, find out how grading, drainage, irrigation, lighting or hardscape features may relate to one another. A short hiring checklist

Before agreeing to a project, consider these points:

  • Explain your main goal and point out the parts of the yard involved.
  • Ask what site preparation is expected, including grading or clearing if needed.
  • Confirm how you’ll communicate with the team as the work moves along.
  • Discuss any irrigation, drainage or access concerns that could affect the plan.
  • For sod, ask how the yard will be prepared and what care it will need afterward.

You don’t need to arrive with a complete design. A clear description of how you use the yard gives the conversation a useful starting point. Hearn Landscape is family-owned, and the same team member works with you from the first estimate through the completed project.

Mistakes that can lead to frustration

Choosing a service based only on the visible finish can leave important work out of the conversation. Fresh sod, for example, depends on proper grading and soil preparation. A patio or retaining wall may also need attention to the ground around it. Ask what work is part of the plan, rather than assuming every step is included.

Another common mistake is treating each yard problem as unrelated. A low spot, a planned walkway and an irrigation line can affect one another. Share what you know about the area and ask the landscaper to explain any connections before work begins. It’s also worth mentioning access needs, existing features you want to keep, and areas that should remain undisturbed.

When to bring in a professional

Call a professional when you’re unsure why water is moving across the property, when a slope needs shaping, or when a project involves several features that must fit together. Land clearing, brush removal, grading and drainage can require more than a simple cleanup. A landscaper can look at the site, discuss your priorities and help define a reasonable scope.

Before an estimate, gather a few photos, note any trouble spots and think about your preferred use for the space.
